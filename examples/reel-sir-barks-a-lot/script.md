## Script (narrator)

In my world, your pet becomes whatever you name it.
Mrs. Lee named her cat Fluffy. It's ninety percent fluff now. You can lose a shoe in it.
The kid next door named his hamster Godzilla. It lives at the zoo now. Behind three fences.
My uncle named his goldfish Lucky. It has won the lottery twice.
When I was four, my parents let me name our puppy.
I named him Sir Barks-A-Lot.
I was four.
Sir Barks-A-Lot has not stopped barking since.
He barks when he eats. He barks in his sleep. He barked through my whole birthday, including the candles.
Our neighbor Mr. Greer put a sign in his yard. Quiet please. Then a bigger sign. QUIET PLEASE.
Then a sign that just said, why.
My little sister Juniper is three. She names everything.
Her shoes are named Kevin. Both of them.
One night in winter, the power went out across the whole town. Then the snow came in sideways.
And Juniper was gone.
Her boots were gone from the door. Both Kevins.
She'd gone out to see the snow. In the dark.
Everybody came out with flashlights. Mr. Greer too.
But the snow was so thick you couldn't see your own hand.
Flashlights didn't help. Shouting didn't help. The wind ate every word.
Except one sound.
Sir Barks-A-Lot ran into the dark, barking. And barking. And barking.
And we could hear exactly where he was.
We followed the barking past the school, past the frozen pond, into the trees.
Then the barking changed. Short and loud, in one spot.
He was standing over Juniper, curled up in the snow behind a log, holding a stick she had named Gary.
He barked at her the whole way home so she couldn't fall asleep.
Mr. Greer carried her.
The next morning Mr. Greer took down every one of his signs.
The week after that, he got a puppy.
He named it Sir Barks-A-Lot the Second.
Now half the dogs on our street have that name.
It's very loud here.
Juniper renamed our dog Hero.
It didn't take. He's still Sir Barks-A-Lot.
Some names are just right the first time.
